Arm a rule with one XRPL payment

You keep your keys. There is no wallet to connect, nothing to install, and no FLR to hold. This page builds the payment locally in your browser and then shows you, in plain English, exactly what signing it will authorise.

The XRP here is testnet XRP and is not worth anything. Everything below behaves exactly as it would with real money, which is the point of showing it to you.

Your XRPL account

Read-only. This asks Flare which contract address your XRPL account controls.

The rule

What you would be authorising

Read this before you send anything. An XRPL payment cannot be recalled. The 42-byte memo below is the only thing that decides what happens on Flare, and it is unreadable by eye — so this is it, decoded independently from the bytes themselves rather than from what was typed above.

Then tell the enclave your price

This payment does not carry your secret price, and it cannot. Anything in the payment is on a public ledger forever. Arming creates the rule with no threshold at all; the number is delivered separately, encrypted to the enclave's own key, so that nobody — including us — can read it.

After the payment settles and your rule exists, run this once. It encrypts your price to the enclave and commits only a hash of it on chain.

Send it

Make this payment from your XRPL wallet. All three fields matter, and the memo most of all.

Do not add a destination tag. A registered tag overrides the memo entirely and credits the tag holder instead — the payment would be silently donated to a stranger.

Built by this page in your browser — no server sees your rule. The encoder is cross-checked byte-for-byte against the command-line builder in arming/bin/arm.dart using a payment that settled on chain; if the two ever disagreed, the test suite would fail rather than a payment going somewhere unintended.

Not one-tap yet. Xaman's hex deep link carries TrustSet transactions only, so a memo-bearing sign request needs their Platform API and a server-held key. That is the one piece of this flow that cannot be done from a static page, and it is not built.
